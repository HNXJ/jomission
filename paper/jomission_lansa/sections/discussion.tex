\section{Discussion}

A column sized from LANSA cell densities, with an imposed SOM/PV ramp, placed alpha-beta in its deep half on its
first seeds but left relative gamma just above mid-L4 at a quarter to a third of the observed level. No run passed
the scorer, including the setting selected by the pre-registered search when rerun on fresh seeds, and that
setting also lost the reference's deep alpha-beta on fresh seeds (0.83--0.86 on the reference's 8 s seeds, 0.35--0.47
on the setting's 12 s fresh seeds). Deep alpha-beta is therefore
reproduced only by the unsearched reference, in-sample (it was not rerun on fresh seeds), and the search, which selected on whole-map similarity,
did not preserve it.

The negative result is specific. It holds for this three-unit E/PV/SOM column with density-scaled sizes and
in-degrees, the declared SOM/PV ramp, the tested SOM$\to$E kinetics and network sizes, and stationary drive. It does
not show that density-based or CAMINOS-based models in general cannot produce the motif, and it does not test the
PV$\to$E kinetics that \citet{Herrera2026} found to control layer-4 gamma. It shows that densities and SOM
kinetics alone were not sufficient here. The controls show that the reference map is fragile to either
construction input, in opposite directions: changing the SOM/PV ramp handed the map to the superficial unit and
changing the density profile handed it to the deep unit. Neither moved a single band, so the controls do not
identify a band-specific mechanism for either input, and both change sizes, in-degrees and rates along with the
named quantity. The ramp was declared and runs against the measured CB/PV profile, so the model's deep alpha-beta
rests on an assumption whose reversal breaks it.

\textbf{What is missing.} The isolated CAMINOS module oscillates at 27--46 Hz, below the scored 50--150 Hz band,
and in the stack the M and D units peak at 11--12 Hz. The model may therefore lack a fast oscillatory mode in the
scored band at any depth, rather than place one at the wrong depth: its 50--150 Hz power comes from the tails of
these rhythms and from S. Because the map is relative, the deficit is M's gamma being small compared with S's, not
a measured shortfall of absolute power, and excess S power contributes to it. The exploratory weight runs suggest
that in M gamma and alpha-beta come from the same recurrent excitation: raising M's E$\to$E weight lifted the
gamma correlation but moved the alpha-beta peak to M, and adding PV$\to$E inhibition restored deep alpha-beta at
the cost of M's gamma. Inferred from hand-chosen single-seed runs, this is a hypothesis. It predicts that a change
which raises M's gamma at a fixed M firing rate, for example in PV kinetics or PV$\to$PV coupling with the E rate
matched, would raise $r_{gm}$ without breaking the crossing, and that a rate-matched control without the gamma
change would not. In the mouse V1 model of \citet{Herrera2026} the temporal properties of layer-4 PV-to-excitatory
synapses were especially influential and gamma (50--80 Hz) was generated mainly in layer 4, which corresponds to
our M unit; a primate column may need such a generator with gamma that extends above layer 4, as the observed map
shows. That test needs its own seal and is not run here. The readout comparison gives it a quantitative target: the
circuit puts 17--19\% of its gamma power in M with real-part source coherences of at most 0.27, while the readout's constrained
optimum puts 49\% there with coherent sources.

\textbf{The readout.} The RWS-kernel readout is not a calibrated potential. It approximates the quasi-static
volume-conductor solution with a current-based proxy whose weights explained over 90\% of the variance of
multicompartment LFPs \citep{Mazzoni2015}, combined with an assumed separable depth kernel. A spectral gain common
to all units and depths cancels in the per-frequency normalization (Methods); receptor- and unit-dependent
filtering does not, and is unvalidated for this model and for the macaque LFP. Of the uncalibrated parts we tested
the spatial ones. The kernel width and unit depths both change the band correlations, and neither removes the
mid-depth deficit. Under this readout the circuit's failure of the gamma depth criterion reflects its source
spectra, because the same readout can meet that criterion without coherence; the statement is conditional on the
three-source observation model and says nothing observation-independent about cortical generators. The readout is
also a confound: moving only the contact depths nearly doubled $r_\text{proto}$ and the spectral-shift null was not
rejected, and probe tilt changed the laminar contrast metric without raising $r_\text{proto}$. A similarity score
between a model map and a recorded map is weak evidence of shared generators unless it beats spectrum-shift and
identical-unit nulls on the same geometry.

\textbf{Limits.} The model is stationary while the target is not: every unit receives the same stationary Poisson
drive, whereas the observed maps come from 1 s peri-stimulus windows in which layers receive different, timed
inputs, so part of the mismatch may reflect drive rather than circuit. The column has three units, so depth
resolution comes from the readout kernel, not from the model. LANSA densities are relative per marker, so they set
depth profiles, not ratios across types, and calbindin is not a specific SOM marker. The SOM/PV ramp was declared,
not measured, and the controls separate it from the density profile only at three seeds per condition, without
correction across outcomes. VIP cells are absent because the AdEx kernel has no VIP type. The pre-registered tests
of E/I balance, SOM/PV scaling and PV or SOM silencing (Table~\ref{tab:notrun}) were not run. The target map is the
LANSA mean; the motif's generality is itself disputed \citep{Mackey2026}, and our conclusions are conditional on
that target. In the mouse V1 model of \citet{Herrera2026}, SOM cells also shaped low-frequency power, which fits
the deep alpha-beta of our SOM-rich D unit, but species, cell types and drive differ, so the correspondence is
qualitative.
