\section{Methods}

\subsection{Model}

The model is a three-unit stack (S, M, D) of AdEx populations E, PV and SOM per unit (CAMINOS;
\rec{C1}). Cell and synapse parameters come from the JaxFNE \texttt{PORT\_SPEC}
tables of the published module \citep{Tahvili2025,Tahvili2026}; PING is the gamma mechanism assumed for the E--PV
loop \citep{Borgers2003}.

\textbf{Within a unit.} The published module has 8000 E and 1500 inhibitory cells with connection probability
0.07. A unit may be smaller. Without the in-degree rule below, a unit keeps the published in-degree (560 E and 105
inhibitory inputs per cell) through $p = 0.07 \cdot 8000/N_E$; with it (every stack run here), each unit's in-degree
scales with its density (below), with mean 560 over units at $B = 1$. E:I is 8000:1500 in every unit. The inhibitory cells split into PV
and SOM by $\rho = N_\text{SOM}/N_\text{PV}$: 0.25 (S), 1 (M), 2 (D). Synapses are conductance-based, AMPA decay
2 ms and GABA decay 8 ms, with a 2 ms delay. Each E and PV cell receives $\text{round}(0.07 \cdot 8000) = 560$
private Poisson inputs at 4 Hz (weight 2.5 nS); SOM cells receive no external drive.

\textbf{Between units.} Adjacent units are coupled both ways from E onto E, PV and SOM with the local weights and
the 2 ms delay, at an in-degree of $K_\text{INTER} = 80$ per neighbour; an end unit takes $2K_\text{INTER}$ from its
one neighbour, so every cell receives 160 inter-unit E inputs.

\textbf{Modifiers.} A layout name lists its modifiers. \texttt{n}$N$: $N$ E cells per unit on average.
\texttt{dens}: E cells per unit proportional to $D_u$, the LANSA density summed over PV, CB, CR and NG and over the
unit's depth bins (median over areas). The sum is a size proxy only, since each marker's density is relative to its
own maximum, so the model is density-informed, not density-derived: unit sizes and the in-degree scaling come
from LANSA; the SOM/PV ramp, E:I, weights, unit depths and inter-unit coupling are imposed. \texttt{kd}$B$: local in-degree scaled by
density. Together,
\begin{equation*}
N_{E,u} = 3N\,\frac{D_u}{\sum_v D_v}, \qquad K_u = K_E\Bigl(\frac{D_u}{\bar D}\Bigr)^{B}, \qquad
p_u = \frac{K_u}{N_{E,u}},
\end{equation*}
with $\bar D$ the mean of $D_u$ over units, $K_E = 560$ and counts rounded to integers. Without \texttt{kd},
$K_u = K_E$ and $p_u = 0.07\cdot 8000/N_{E,u}$. With $B = 1$, $K_u \propto N_{E,u}$, so $p_u = K_E/N = 0.14$ in
every unit at $N = 4000$ (Table~S4); with $B = 1.41$, $p_u$ is 0.163, 0.135 and 0.116 at S, M and D (Fig.~\ref{fig:schema}D).
Neither rule is an anatomical measurement: denser units receiving more local synapses is an assumption that T1
varied. \texttt{sst}$NN$: SOM$\to$E decay
$NN$ ms in every unit, weight scaled by $8/NN$ so the charge per spike is unchanged; \texttt{sstd}$NN$: the same in
D only. \texttt{w}$RUX$: rule $R$'s weight in unit $U$ times $X$ (\texttt{ee} E$\to$E, \texttt{es} E$\to$SOM,
\texttt{ep} E$\to$PV, \texttt{pe} PV$\to$E). The reference is \texttt{e2\_3unit\_n4000\_dens\_k1\_kd1\_sst15};
Tables~S1--S4 give its counts, edges and parameters with sources.

\textbf{Software.} All runs use JaxFNE with $dt = 0.1$ ms; the version is read from each run record. E0, E1 and the
$N = 4000$ seed-1 reference ran on a 0.5.9 side install with dense delivery; the other ladder runs and T1 axes 1--2
on 0.5.8, dense; T1 axis 3, the confirmation and the weight runs on 0.5.9 at commit 351f6048 (unreleased) with event delivery.
The software authority is 0.5.8; 0.5.8 and 0.5.9 gave identical per-step spike counts on the $N = 4000$ stack over
0.5 s (seal \rec{S3}). E2 is declared exploration except where a seal is named.
Twenty-four of the run records carry a dirty-working-tree flag beside their commit (all 15 T8 runs, the 3
confirmation runs, 4 of the 21 deep-unit-decay runs and 2 weight runs). The flag records that the checkout held
uncommitted changes when the run started; the records do not enumerate them, so the paper cannot state their scope.
Every file the paper cites is committed and hashed in \rec{D6}, and the analysis scripts that read these outputs are
at the commits in Table~\ref{tab:records}.

\textbf{Event-driven delivery.} The event route computes AdEx synaptic input only from presynaptic cells that
spiked in the step. It was compared with the dense route on all 22 saved output arrays in two layouts: one 8 s T1
run (\texttt{kd1p41}, seed 1; 248\,116 spikes, equal edge tables) and the three T8 reference runs
(\texttt{e2\_3unit\_n4000\_dens\_k1\_kd1\_sst15}, seeds 1--3; \rec{D9}). The event runs used JaxFNE 0.5.9 at
351f6048; the dense runs they were compared with used 0.5.8, except reference seed 1 (0.5.9 side install). All
arrays were bitwise equal; as negative controls, a different layout differed in 21 of 22 arrays, and different
seeds of one layout in 21 of 22. One timed pair on one machine took 140 s against 1387 s dense (9.9-fold;
\rec{N1}~\S5); the speed-up is specific to that configuration. T1 axis 3, the confirmation, the weight runs and
T8 used this route; the ladder and T1 axes 1--2 used the dense route. Runs on other layouts and at 12 s rely
on these checks; equality was not shown for them.

\subsection{Single-module tests E0 and E1}

\textbf{E0} (seal \rec{S1}) builds the published module (8000 E, 750 PV, 750 SOM, $p = 0.07$,
no PV$\to$SOM or SOM$\to$SOM edges) and runs seeds 1--5 for 12 s at $dt = 0.1$ ms, discarding 2 s. JaxFNE's own
CAMINOS analysis, unchanged, gives the E-population peak frequency $f_\text{pk}$ (1 s Welch windows, 1 Hz bins), the
PV-to-SOM lag and the mean rates. References and margins are JaxFNE's: $f_\text{pk}$ 33.8 Hz $\pm$ 1 Hz, lag 7.04
$\pm$ 0.34 ms, rates 1.106, 11.37 and 7.05 Hz $\pm$ 6.1, 1.2 and 8.2\%. PASS needs all five seed means inside their
margins; a rate-matched Poisson raster scored by the same analysis must not oscillate.

\textbf{E1} (seal \rec{S2}) keeps E0 fixed and sets $\rho = N_\text{SOM}/N_\text{PV}$ to 0.25,
0.5, 1, 2 and 4 at $N_\text{PV} + N_\text{SOM} = 1500$, seeds 1--3. A run oscillates when its spectral peak excess
is at least 4; otherwise $f_\text{pk}$ is undefined. Criteria: (1) at least 4 of 5 $\rho$ oscillate in each seed;
(2) the fitted slope of $f_\text{pk}$ on $\log_2\rho$ over the oscillating points is negative in every
seed; (3) $f_\text{pk}$ drops by at least 3 Hz from $\rho$ 0.25 to 4; (4) one-sided permutation $p < 0.05$ for
the mean slope, permuting $\rho$ labels within seeds (10\,000 random draws, a Monte Carlo test whose resolution
is $1/10\,001$). Failing (1) or an undefined criterion gives
INCONCLUSIVE. The $\rho = 1$ runs reuse E0 seeds 1--3 (same configuration), so only 12 of the 15 runs are new.

\subsection{Readouts}

\textbf{From transmembrane currents to the LFP.} At LFP frequencies the extracellular medium is quasi-static: the
displacement and inductive terms of Maxwell's equations are negligible, so the potential obeys Poisson's equation
with the transmembrane currents as sources, and in an ohmic, homogeneous medium of conductivity $\sigma_e$
\begin{equation*}
\phi(\mathbf{r},t) = \frac{1}{4\pi\sigma_e}\sum_n \frac{I_n(t)}{|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_n|},
\end{equation*}
a sum over all membrane segments $n$. \citet{Mazzoni2015} computed their reference LFP of multicompartment
pyramidal cells with the line-source form of this volume-conduction sum. Two properties make a current-based proxy defensible. First,
$\phi$ is linear in the $I_n$, and if synaptic inputs are treated as current sources into passive dendrites, the
$I_n$ are linear filters of those currents (the cable equation is linear); with conductance-based synapses this
holds to the extent that driving-force fluctuations are small. The potential at a contact at depth $z$ is then
\begin{equation*}
\phi_z(t) = \sum_u \sum_{r\in\{\text{AMPA},\text{GABA}\}} \bigl(h_{z,u,r} * I_{u,r}\bigr)(t),
\end{equation*}
where $I_{u,r}$ is the total synaptic current of receptor $r$ onto unit $u$'s pyramidal cells and $h_{z,u,r}$ is
a spatiotemporal kernel set by morphology, synapse placement, return currents and distance. Second, in the multicompartment network of
\citet{Mazzoni2015} the contribution of the interneurons was negligible (their Figs.~3 and 8: stellate
dendrites with symmetrically placed synapses form a closed field), so the LFP is set by the synaptic currents onto pyramidal cells,
including the GABA currents that interneurons supply. They reported that a weighted sum of delayed currents,
$c_\text{AMPA} = 1$, $c_\text{GABA} = -1.65$, $\tau_\text{AMPA} = 6$ ms and $\tau_\text{GABA} = 0$ (the RWS proxy),
explained more than 90\% of the variance of the multicompartment LFP time course over the recording positions
they tested, including depths along the column axis (their Fig.~5), performing poorly only where the LFP itself
was small, such as near its inversion point; that the proxies predicted the LFP gamma peak frequency (their
Fig.~7C); and that they tended to underestimate low frequencies and overestimate gamma (their Fig.~7A--B). The
weights were fitted to their morphology, synapse placement and positions ($\alpha = 1.65$ is the mean over depths of
the optimal weight). Using them as a
separable kernel, $h_{z,u,r}(t) = w_{zu}\,c_r\,\delta(t-\tau_r)$, with one spatial weight $w_{zu}$ shared by AMPA
and GABA at every depth, is our assumption: real AMPA and GABA sources sit at different places on the cell, so
their contributions differ in profile and can differ in sign above and below the soma. The 90\% figure
validates the proxy against Mazzoni et al.'s own multicompartment model; it is external evidence, not a
validation of the proxy for this model's cells and synapses or for the macaque LFP.

\textbf{LFP proxy and probe used here.} We use RWS per unit, $L_u(t) = |I_{\text{AMPA},u}(t - 6\,\text{ms})| -
1.65\,|I_{\text{GABA},u}(t)|$, summed over the unit's E cells, and the spatial weights $w_{zu} = K_{zu} =
\exp\bigl(-(z-z_u)^2/2\sigma^2\bigr)$, $\sigma = 0.15$ (ASSUMED), on 21 contacts from $-0.6$ (top) to $+0.4$
(bottom); unit depths are S $-0.4$, M 0, D $+0.3$. Currents are taken at $V_\text{ref} = -65$ mV, so $I_{u,r} =
g_{u,r}(V_\text{ref} - E_r)$ with $E_\text{AMPA} = 0$ and $E_\text{GABA} = -80$ mV; both currents keep one sign, the
absolute values only fix the sign convention, and $L_u = 65\,g_{\text{AMPA},u}(t-6\,\text{ms}) - 24.75\,
g_{\text{GABA},u}(t)$ (pA, with $g$ in nS and the coefficients in mV) is, by construction of the proxy, exactly linear in the conductances. Hence the contact power is exactly
\begin{equation*}
P_z(f) = \sum_{u,v} K_{zu} K_{zv}\, S_{uv}(f),
\end{equation*}
with $S_{uv}$ the cross-spectra of the $L_u$. The scored map divides by the maximum over depth at each frequency,
$R(z,f) = P_z(f)/\max_{z'} P_{z'}(f)$, so any frequency-dependent gain $G(f)$ common to all units and
independent of contact depth, such as the proxy's spectral bias above if it is uniform, cancels exactly
(depth-dependent filtering, which the separable kernel already neglects, would not): $G(f)P_z(f)/\max_{z'}
G(f)P_{z'}(f) = R(z,f)$. What the relative map does depend on is the depth weighting $K$, i.e.\ $\sigma$ and the unit
depths, and filtering that differs between receptors or units: the AMPA/GABA weight and delay set the interference
within each $L_u$, and they remain unvalidated for this model. We therefore report the readout as an estimate of
the LFP's depth structure, not a calibrated potential, and test its two spatial parts: unit depths (Results, depth placement)
and $\sigma$ (Results; $\sigma \in \{0.10, 0.15, 0.20, 0.25\}$, \rec{C4}).

\textbf{CSD.} The CSD is current-based, not kernel-based. The saved per-unit, per-receptor currents become current
sources at declared depths, each with an equal return current. Placements are ASSUMED, not measured:
PV perisomatic; SST distal apical; AMPA basal and apical. The forward model gives $\phi$ on the probe, and the CSD
is the second difference of $\phi$ against the true sources. Trigger: average on the alpha-band trough
(stationary runs have no evoked response).

\textbf{Spectra and motif.} Model power is stationary; LANSA power is from 1 s peri-stimulus windows. Bands:
primary alpha-beta 10--30 Hz and gamma 50--150 Hz. The LANSA-style bands 8--30 and 31--150 Hz are scored as a secondary check only; over the 9 ladder and 3 confirmation runs the 31--150 Hz $r_{gm}$ was 0.16--0.48, also below 0.6.

\subsection{Shared scorer}

\textbf{Metrics.} After the 2 s discard the summed RWS signal of each unit is cut into 1 s windows; the cross-spectra
of the units, weighted by the Gaussian contact kernel, give power per contact at 1--150 Hz (1 Hz bins), averaged over
windows. The relative-power map divides each frequency's power by its maximum over the 21 contacts, as for LANSA.
$r_\text{proto}$ is the Pearson correlation over contacts between the model and the observed 11-area map, averaged
over the 150 frequencies. $r_{ab}$ and $r_{gm}$ are Pearson correlations over contacts of the band-mean relative-power
profiles (10--30 and 50--150 Hz) against the observed ones. \texttt{crossing\_2se}: the alpha-beta minus gamma profile
is below $-2$ jackknife SE (over windows) at the top contact and above $+2$ SE at the bottom one. The smooth-spectrum
null gives each unit a random smooth log spectrum (six Gaussian bumps in $\log f$ plus a $-a\log f$ slope, $a \sim
U(0,2)$; 1000 draws, seed 0) and takes the 95th percentile of the resulting $r_\text{proto}$; the identical-unit
control gives every unit unit S's spectrum. Unit-order rank compares $r_\text{proto}$ for all six assignments of the
unit spectra to the three depths. The pass rule was fixed at commit 88d282d (seal \rec{S4}).
What each control keeps and destroys: the smooth-spectrum null keeps the geometry (kernel, depths, normalization) and
replaces the spectra with random smooth ones without cross-terms, so it asks whether any smooth spectra on this
geometry reach the score; the identical-unit control keeps one real spectrum and removes all differences between
units; the unit-order rank keeps the spectra and the geometry and tests their pairing; the spectral-shift null of the
depth-placement analysis keeps each unit's power and spectral shape but moves its frequency content. The 21 $\times$
150 pixels of a map are strongly dependent, so $r_\text{proto}$ is a similarity score, not a test with 3150 samples;
inference uses seeds and the nulls above.

A result counts only if all of the following hold (scorer \rec{C2}):
\begin{enumerate}
\item \texttt{crossing\_2se} is true on the sum-over-cells row and verdict bands.
\item $r_{ab} \geq 0.6$ and $r_{gm} \geq 0.6$.
\item The identity unit order ranks 1 of all unit orders (ties count for the identity).
\item $r_\text{proto}$ is above both the p95 of the max-over-grid smooth-spectrum null and the identical-unit control.
\item The two halves agree: $|r_1 - r_2| \leq 2\,\text{SE}_\text{diff}$, with jackknife SEs over windows in each half. This is
a consistency check: passing it means no difference was detected, not that the run is stationary.
\item The result is finite and complete: all 150 frequencies enter $r_\text{over\,bins}$.
\end{enumerate}
Selection uses seeds 1--3. Confirmation uses seeds 4--6 at 12 s, with the config frozen. The even/odd area split
is not a hold-out.

\subsection{Readout capacity}

These analyses use no simulation (\rec{C5}--\rec{C7}, \rec{C10}, \rec{C11}, \rec{C15}). Geometry: the 21 contacts,
the three unit depths and the Gaussian kernel $K$. A source model gives contact power $P_z = k_z^\top C k_z$ for a
3 $\times$ 3 source covariance $C$ (one frequency, or one covariance for a band-mean profile), and the scored
quantity is $R_z = P_z/\max_{z'} P_{z'}$; ``mid'' is the mean of $R$ over the five contacts from $-0.2$ to 0.
Targets: the observed 50--150 Hz band-mean profile divided by its maximum, $y$ (mid 0.946), and the mean of the
observed per-frequency map over 50--150 Hz and the mid contacts (0.848).

\textbf{Parameterization.} $C = LL^\top$ with $L$ real lower-triangular (six free entries), so every $C$ is
symmetric positive semidefinite (PSD) by construction. With a real kernel only $\mathrm{Re}\,C$ enters $P$, and
the real part of a Hermitian PSD cross-spectral matrix is PSD, so no admissible spectral covariance is excluded.
Without cross terms $C$ is diagonal with entries $a_u^2$ (three free entries). Each reported optimum carries the
eigenvalues of $C$ over its trace; the smallest lies between $-4\times10^{-19}$ and $9\times10^{-4}$, so the $r$
maxima are rank one at $\sigma \leq 0.20$ and lie on the boundary of the PSD set.

\textbf{Objectives.} (i) Maximum mid at one frequency. (ii) Maximum Pearson $r(P, y)$, full or diagonal $C$. (iii)
Joint: maximum $r(P, y)$ subject to $|\text{mid}(P) - m| \leq \varepsilon$, $\varepsilon = 0.02$, for $m$ = 0.946
and 0.848, minimized as $-r + 100\max(0, |\text{mid} - m| - \varepsilon)$; a solution is feasible when it meets the
constraint to $10^{-9}$. Optimizer: Nelder--Mead (\texttt{xatol} $10^{-10}$, \texttt{fatol} $10^{-13}$, at most
8000 iterations) from 48 standard-normal starts, best objective kept; seed 20261110 (the checks below use seed 20261111).

\textbf{Checks.} Self-recovery: five profiles generated by random $C$ on the three sources were recovered at
$r \geq 0.99999$ at each width. Negative control: on an alternating 1/0 profile over the contacts, finer than every
kernel, $r$ stayed at 0.07--0.14. Order: maximum $r$ was never below that of a least-squares fit of $y$ (0.45 at
$\sigma = 0.15$; least squares understates the reachable $r$) or of the diagonal case. For the joint objective
(\rec{C15}, \rec{D10}), four synthetic targets made by the readout itself (two rank one, two full rank) with $m$
equal to their own mid were recovered feasible at $r \geq 0.9999$, and an unattainable $m = 1.2$ was returned
infeasible. Independent of Nelder--Mead, a Fibonacci grid of $2\times10^5$ rank-one covariances $C = uu^\top$ gave
the constrained maximum $r$ 0.8450 at $\sigma = 0.15$ and $m = 0.848$ (optimizer 0.8452), and at $\sigma = 0.20$
0.9775 and 0.9814 for $m$ = 0.946 and 0.848 (optimizer 0.9779 and 0.9814); $2\times10^5$ random full-rank $L$ gave
at most 0.8423 at 0.15. No grid point reached $m = 0.946$ at 0.15 (largest rank-one mid 0.852). The diagonal case
was checked on a simplex grid of power shares with step 1/400 (\rec{C11}, \rec{D7}). Grids and samples are lower
bounds on the constrained maximum, so the joint optimum is a numerical solution, not a proven global maximum.

\textbf{Distributed model.} 41 sources from $-0.6$ to 0.4 (step 0.025) with non-negative weights fitted by NNLS
reached $r$ 0.997--0.999; with 41 weights for 21 contacts this is a capacity check, not a validation.

\textbf{Cross-area generalization.} For each of the 11 areas, the covariance is fitted on the mean profile of the
other 10 and its profile correlated with the held-out area (\rec{C6}); the baseline correlates the 10-area mean
profile itself with the held-out area. Signed lead fields, receptor-specific source depths and depth-dependent
filtering are not modelled. The main-text width $\sigma = 0.15$ is the width declared in the readout code and used
for the T1 seal's ceilings before any T1 run (\rec{S4}); the other widths were added after review and are reported,
not selected.

\subsection{Finite-size ladder}

Lineage L-FS (seal \rec{S3}). The question is the smallest $N$ E cells per unit at which
\texttt{kd1\_sst15} keeps its map. The unit-specific in-degree rule ($K_u = 560\,D_u/\bar D$, $B = 1$) and $K_\text{INTER}$
are held fixed across $N$; only $p_u$ changes. $N \in \{4000, 2000, 1000\}$,
seeds 1--3, 8 s with 2 s discarded. Rule: accept the smallest $N$ whose seed-mean $r_\text{proto}$ is within
2 seed SD of $N = 4000$ and whose per-unit peaks are within 3 Hz. That $N$ is the T1 size.

The literal 1000-total-cell model (L-FS1k, weight scaling $\times K_\text{ref}/K_\text{real}$) is a separate
lineage and is not run here.

Table~\ref{tab:records} (C code, S seals, D derived data, R run records, N notes), with the commit of each code
and seal file. Code and seals are in the project repository \citep{Jomission}; the simulator is JaxFNE 0.5.9
\citep{JaxFNE}. Seeds are given per run in the tables and run records. The run records are not under version
control, nor are some figures drawn from them (Table~\ref{tab:records}); record \rec{D6} holds the SHA-256 hashes
of 169 files, including every figure shown, so an untracked figure can be checked against the one in this
preprint. Availability is stated under Data availability.
