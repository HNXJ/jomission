\section{Discussion}

A column sized from LANSA cell densities, with an imposed SOM/PV ramp, placed alpha-beta in its deep half on its
first seeds but left relative gamma just above mid-L4 at 0.19--0.31 against 0.85 observed (22--36% of it) across the twelve scored runs (reference alone 0.23--0.26). No run
passed the scorer. The setting selected by the pre-registered search and rerun on fresh seeds never reached the
reference's deep alpha-beta, at 0.32--0.47 on its own selection seeds against the reference's 0.83--0.86, nor on
the fresh seeds (0.35--0.47). Deep alpha-beta occurred in searched levels: 17 of the 54 new T1 simulations had
$r_{ab}$ at least the reference's lowest seed value, and the largest $r_{gm}$ among them was 0.35, still below the 0.6 threshold. No tested setting
satisfied the joint criterion; the hand-chosen middle-unit weight settings, run on one seed, also failed it. Of the settings carried forward, only the unsearched reference kept both bands in view. The
search selected on whole-map similarity, and its deep-unit decay level (21 ms) reached
$r_{gm}$ 0.48--0.50 there, still below 0.6, and kept only $r_{ab}$ 0.32--0.47 on its selection seeds.

The negative result is specific. It holds for this three-unit E/PV/SOM column with density-scaled sizes and
in-degrees, the declared SOM/PV ramp, the tested SOM$\to$E kinetics and network sizes, and stationary drive. It does
not show that density-based or CAMINOS-based models in general cannot produce the motif, and it does not test the
PV$\to$E kinetics that the model of \citet{Herrera2026} implicates in layer-4 gamma, as its abstract reports. It
shows that densities and SOM
kinetics alone were not sufficient here. The controls show that the reference map is fragile to either
construction input, in opposite directions: changing the SOM/PV ramp handed the map to the superficial unit and
changing the density profile handed it to the deep unit. Neither moved a single band, so the controls do not
identify a band-specific mechanism for either input, and both change sizes, in-degrees and rates along with the
named quantity. The ramp was declared and runs against the measured CB/PV profile, so the model's deep alpha-beta
rests on an assumption whose reversal breaks it.

\textbf{What is missing.} The isolated CAMINOS module oscillates at 26--47 Hz for $\rho \leq 2$ (E1) and at 24--26 Hz at $\rho = 4$ in E1b arm B, below the scored 50--150 Hz band,
and in the stack the M and D units peak at 11--12 Hz. These peaks are consistent with a model that lacks a fast
oscillatory mode in the scored band at any depth: its 50--150 Hz power comes from the tails of
these rhythms and from S. Because the map is relative, the deficit is M's gamma being small compared with S's, not
a measured shortfall of absolute power, and excess S power contributes to it. The hand-chosen weight runs are
consistent with gamma and alpha-beta in M coming from the same recurrent excitation: raising M's E$\to$E weight lifted the
gamma correlation but moved the alpha-beta peak to M, and adding PV$\to$E inhibition restored deep alpha-beta at
the cost of M's gamma. Inferred from hand-chosen single-seed runs, this is a hypothesis. It predicts that a change
which raises M's gamma at a fixed M firing rate, for example in PV kinetics or PV$\to$PV coupling with the E rate
matched, would raise $r_{gm}$ without breaking the crossing, and that a rate-matched control without the gamma
change would not. The mouse V1 model of \citet{Herrera2026} implicates layer-4 PV-to-excitatory dynamics in the
motif, according to its abstract; the full text was not available to us, so we do not attribute specific kinetic
results to it. That model corresponds to
our M unit; the observed map, whose gamma extends above layer 4, is consistent with a primate column that needs
such a generator above layer 4 as well. That test needs its own seal and is not run here. The readout comparison gives it a quantitative target: the
circuit puts 17--19\% of its gamma power in M with real-part source coherences of at most 0.27, while the readout's constrained
optimum puts 49\% there with coherent sources.

\textbf{The readout.} The RWS-kernel readout is not a calibrated potential. It approximates the quasi-static
volume-conductor solution with a current-based proxy whose weights explained the variance of
multicompartment LFPs \citep{Mazzoni2015}, combined with an assumed separable depth kernel. A spectral gain common
to all units and depths cancels in the per-frequency normalization (Methods); receptor- and unit-dependent
filtering does not, and is unvalidated for this model and for the macaque LFP. Of the uncalibrated parts we tested
the spatial ones. The kernel width and unit depths both change the band correlations, and neither removes the
mid-depth deficit. The same readout can meet the gamma depth criterion without coherence, so the readout control
implicates model spectra but does not exclude observation-model effects, and it says nothing
observation-independent about cortical generators. The readout is
also a confound: moving only the three unit depths raised $r_\text{proto}$ from 0.38 at the unmoved depths (which fails the $r_{gm}$ threshold) to 0.72, the highest of the 59 of 1,330 placements that met both band thresholds on the selection run, and the spectral-shift null was not
rejected, and probe tilt changed the laminar contrast metric without raising $r_\text{proto}$. A similarity score
between a model map and a recorded map is weak evidence of shared generators unless it beats spectrum-shift and
identical-unit nulls on the same geometry.

\textbf{Limits.} The model is stationary while the target is not: every unit receives the same stationary Poisson
drive, whereas the observed maps come from peri-stimulus windows in which layers receive different, timed
inputs, so the mismatch is consistent with a contribution of drive as well as circuit. The column has three units, so depth
resolution comes from the readout kernel. LANSA densities are relative per marker, so they set
depth profiles and give no ratios across types, and calbindin is not a specific SOM marker. The SOM/PV ramp was declared,
not measured, and the controls separate it from the density profile only at three seeds per condition, without
correction across outcomes. VIP cells are absent because the AdEx kernel has no VIP type. The pre-registered tests
of E/I balance, SOM/PV scaling and PV or SOM silencing (Table~\ref{tab:notrun}) were not run. The target map is the
LANSA mean; the motif's generality is itself disputed \citep{Mackey2026}, and our conclusions are conditional on
that target. \citet{Herrera2026} report that SOM cells shape low-frequency power in a mouse V1 model, which would
fit the deep alpha-beta of our SOM-rich D unit, but we know that only from their abstract, and species, cell types
and drive differ, so the correspondence is
qualitative.
