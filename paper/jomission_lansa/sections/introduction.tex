\section{Introduction}

Laminar recordings across primate cortex show a spectrolaminar motif: relative LFP power in the gamma range is
highest in superficial layers, relative alpha-beta power is highest in deep layers, and the two depth profiles
cross near layer 4 \citep{MendozaHalliday2024}. Whether the motif is ubiquitous is disputed \citep{Mackey2026}. We take it here as an empirical target, the 11-area mean and V1 maps of the laminar
cell-density and spectral data set of \citet{Mulvey2024} (LANSA below), without assuming it holds in every area or recording.

LANSA measured, in 11 macaque areas, the laminar densities of parvalbumin, calbindin and calretinin interneurons
and of pyramidal neurons alongside the LFP, and found that the motif could be predicted from these laminar cellular
gradients by a regression whose coefficients were conserved across areas \citep{Mulvey2024}. A regression does not
say how the gradients would produce the spectra. Mechanistically, feedback inhibition from fast interneurons supports gamma in
E--I networks \citep{Borgers2003}, and conductance-based network and mean-field models with PV and SOM interneurons
assign the two classes distinct roles in gamma oscillations and network stability \citep{Tahvili2025,Tahvili2026};
we call the E/PV/SOM module of these papers CAMINOS. A bio-realistic model of mouse V1 reproduced the motif with alpha-beta
intrinsic to every layer and gamma generated mainly in layer 4 and propagated across layers \citep{Herrera2026};
whether this account carries over to primate cortex, where the observed gamma is broadest above layer 4, is open.

We built the smallest mechanistic model that links the primate densities to the spectra: three CAMINOS units at
superficial, middle and deep depths, sized by the LANSA densities, with an SOM/PV ratio rising with depth, read out
by a kernel probe and scored against the observed map by a single sealed rule. We ask whether this density-built
column reproduces the motif and, where it fails, what it lacks. The column places alpha-beta deep on its selection
seeds but leaves gamma weak between $-0.2$ and 0 of cortical thickness; network size, sealed fine-tuning and
exploratory weight changes do not close the gap, and readout controls show that probe geometry alone can raise the
similarity score without the spectra carrying the observed gamma.
